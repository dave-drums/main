How to use

How to use Groove Lab

  1. Choose a starting pattern from Grooves, or select Empty 16th note groove to start from scratch. On smaller screens, open the settings icon beside BPM to find Grooves, Metronome, Time Signature and Subdivision.
  2. The grid reads from left to right. Each row is a drum or cymbal. Click or tap a cell to add a hit; tap it again to remove it.
  3. Press Start to listen. The button shows elapsed time while playing; press it again to stop. Use − and + or type a BPM (beats per minute) to change the speed. You can edit notes while the groove plays and hear your changes.
  4. Subdivision controls the grid spacing: Eighth note gives fewer positions per beat than Sixteenth note. Metronome adds a steady click. Use Share to copy or send a link that saves your pattern; Print and Download provide other export options.

Editing tips

  • Click a row label, such as Hi-hat, Snare, Kick or Stickings, to open its menu. You can quickly turn a whole bar of notes on or off.
  • Right-click a note for accents, ghost notes, open hi-hats, flams and drags. On a touch screen, press and hold a note to open these options. Rotate your phone for more grid space.
  • Use the key icon above the score to show the drum legend. Undo restores your previous edit, including Delete all. Speed trainer lets you set a starting BPM, target BPM and an increase after a chosen number of repetitions. One repetition plays every bar in your groove.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Space: start/stop playback when the editor background has focus.
  • Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y: undo / redo an edit.
  • ← / →: decrease / increase tempo by 1 BPM when the editor background has focus.
  • Ctrl + hover / Alt + hover: add / remove hits as you move over hi-hat, snare or kick cells.
  • On a focused grid cell: arrow keys move between notes; Space or Enter toggles a hit. Home/End jump to the first/last cell in a row; Ctrl+Home/End jump to the start/end of the pattern.
  • Escape: close the guide or a settings dropdown.

Click inside Groove Lab before using shortcuts. Typing fields keep their normal keyboard behaviour.
